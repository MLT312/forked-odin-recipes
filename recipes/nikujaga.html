Nikujaga (Japanese Meat & Potato Stew)

Nikujaga is a classic Japanese comfort food made from meat, potatoes, and onions stewed in a sweet and savory broth.

Ingredients

  • Main Ingredients
    • Thinly Sliced Beef
    • Waxy Potatoes
    • Cut Carrots
    • Sliced Onions
    • Shirataki Noodles
    • Blanched Snow Peas
    • Cooking Fat
  • Broth & Seasoning
    • Dashi
    • Soy Sauce
    • Mirin
    • Cooking Sake
    • Sugar

Cooking Steps

  1. Prep the Ingredients
    1. Peel the potatoes and cut them into bite-sized chunks, then soak in water for 5 minutes to remove excess starch. Drain well.
    2. Cut the carrots into irregular chunks (rangiri) and slice the onion into wedges.
    3. If using shirataki noodles, boil them in water for 2 minutes to remove any odor, drain, and cut into shorter lengths.
  2. Sauté the Onion and Beef
    1. Heat the cooking oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat.
    2. Add the onion and sauté until slightly softened or translucent (about 2–3 minutes).
    3. Add the sliced beef and cook gently until it changes color and is no longer pink. (Tip: Some cooks remove the beef here and return it later so it stays tender.)
  3. Add Vegetables and Simmer with Aromatics
    1. Add the drained potatoes, carrots, and shirataki noodles to the pot. Stir gently to coat everything in oil.
    2. Pour in the dashi stock, sake, mirin, and sugar. Bring the liquid to a boil and skim off any foam or scum from the surface.
  4. Cover with a Drop Lid (Otoshi-buta)
    1. Place an otoshi-buta (Japanese drop lid or a round piece of parchment paper with a hole in the center) directly over the ingredients.
    2. Reduce the heat to medium-low and simmer for about 15 to 20 minutes until the potatoes and carrots are tender.
  5. Season with Soy Sauce
    1. Pour in the soy sauce and gently stir or tilt the pot so the flavor distributes without breaking the potato chunks.
    2. Remove the drop lid and simmer uncovered for another 5 minutes until the broth slightly reduces and glazes the ingredients.
  6. Rest Before Serving
    1. Turn off the heat and let the stew rest (covered or uncovered) for 30 to 60 minutes. Nikujaga tastes best as it cools down, allowing the dashi and sweet-savory broth to soak deep into the potatoes and meat.
    2. Reheat gently, top with blanched green beans or snow peas just before serving, and dish it out warm.
